## My view of the project today

The authoritative server and deterministic engine are a promising play-chip foundation. The repo documents cryptographic shuffling, hand commitments, state isolation, outcome replay, chip-conservation checks, and tests for evaluator and pot behavior. `progress.md` also records a successful live two-human hand and cashout. Those are useful engineering results, but the recent missing-migration outage showed that release reliability still needs work. `COMPLIANCE.md` calls its evidence a self-assessment, and `SECURITY-REVIEW.md` identifies gaps in external audit anchoring, wallet accounting, anti-collusion, KYC, and responsible gaming. I would not describe the current product as real-money ready or externally certified.

The owner's stated destination is **human-only online poker with real-money play, mainly crypto and also cards, under a Malta jurisdiction**. Malta classifies peer-to-peer poker as Type 3 under a B2C gaming service licence. The specific countries whose residents may play still need a legal market-by-market decision; a Malta setup alone does not settle every destination market's requirements. The Malta Gaming Authority (MGA) requires prior approval for accepted crypto assets, including flows where a player's crypto is converted to fiat before the operator receives it. Its DLT policy also treats fiat and each accepted virtual financial asset separately and does not permit the gaming platform to serve as an exchange between them.

## The order I would choose

1. **Establish a trustworthy play-chip release.** Make schema migrations part of deployment; fail before accepting players if a required migration is absent. Add a staging smoke test covering two human accounts creating and joining a table, multiple hands, reconnect, leave, cashout, and conserved balances. Exercise mobile, slow network, long disconnects, multi-device sessions, and concurrent actions. Set measurable targets for action latency and stalled-hand recovery, then monitor them in the deployed environment. This is my first priority because money would magnify the impact of the failures users recently experienced.
2. **Freeze and independently challenge the poker rules.** Write a precise rules document for blinds, missed blinds, button movement, short all-ins, side pots, odd chips, timeouts, disconnections, and interrupted hands. Compare the implementation with independent reference tests and high-load multi-table scenarios. Preserve a replayable hand history that support staff and an external lab can examine. Changes to the engine or RNG in a regulated deployment may require MGA approval or recertification, so rules and versioning should be deliberate.
3. **Build money as its own controlled system.** Replace the play-chip bankroll concept with an append-only, balanced ledger for deposits, wagers, rake, prizes, reversals, withdrawals, and fees. Use idempotent payment events and daily reconciliation against bank, payment-provider, and approved crypto-custody records. Keep player funds separately identifiable and withdrawable according to the applicable rules. Choose the card payment provider and crypto custody/payment architecture with compliance specialists before wiring UI buttons to payments. Do not let a poker hand depend on a live card or blockchain settlement call.
4. **Build player protection and game integrity together.** Add identity and age verification, access restrictions for approved player markets, AML/CFT risk assessment and transaction monitoring, deposit or wagering limits, reality checks, self-exclusion, complaint handling, and withdrawal review. Add poker-specific detection for multi-accounting, collusion, soft play, chip dumping, and unauthorized bots, with a player report path and a human investigation workflow. New cash tables should have human seats only; any operator-funded player would require explicit disclosure and controls.
5. **Prepare evidence for regulators and independent reviewers.** Maintain secure change control, access logs, key custody, backup and restore drills, incident response, independent penetration testing, and an externally witnessed or write-once anchor for hand-audit hashes. Use the MGA system documentation, audit, and review checklists and GLI-19 as review inputs, then commission a qualified lab to examine the production-equivalent engine, RNG, and deployment. Do not claim certification until it has actually been granted.

## What would make me change this order

If the project owner already has an MGA-licensed operating partner, approved payment providers, and established KYC/AML and player-funds systems, integrating with those could move ahead of building equivalents. If the target is a software supplier rather than the B2C operator, the licence and responsibility split would also change the plan. A regulator, licensed partner, legal adviser, or independent test lab may identify requirements that supersede my technical priorities.
